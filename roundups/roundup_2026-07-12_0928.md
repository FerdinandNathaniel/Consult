## What Were Followed Accounts Discussing This Week?

**AI Boom Sustainability and Financial Risk**: Experts including [Gary Marcus](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2074208864124445120) raised persistent concerns about the economic viability of current AI investment patterns. Marcus highlighted that [without massive job displacement, AI investments cannot generate the 7% annual productivity gains needed to earn out](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2074208864124445120), making current spending unsustainable. Reports emerged that [the U.S. Treasury Department has completed an internal report comparing the AI boom to the dotcom crash and warning of systemic financial risk](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2074292954978783352), though the report was reportedly withheld from public acknowledgment. By week's end, [major technology companies were increasingly turning to debt markets to fund AI infrastructure, with Amazon, Alphabet, Nvidia, Meta, Oracle, and SpaceX raising $182 billion in bonds in 2026](https://x.com/Schuldensuehner/status/2074910617891201310), though investor appetite was beginning to crack. Simultaneously, [Germany faced economic vulnerability—potentially losing 1.2% of annual GDP in 2027 if an AI sector correction occurs](https://x.com/Schuldensuehner/status/2074746809453338638), despite minimal gains from the AI boom thus far.

**AI Training Data and Copyright Policy**: The week surfaced contradictory positions on copyright and AI training. [Anthropic sought massive copyright exemptions for training Claude while simultaneously opposing Chinese IP theft](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2074332988410613869), while [Hugging Face faced lawsuits for distributing copyrighted images used in AI training despite year-long warnings](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2074130273298686081). Later in the week, [Meta's Muse Image model raised direct EU AI Act and GDPR compliance questions by using Instagram photos without consent via an opt-out model](https://the-decoder.com/muse-image-is-technically-impressive-but-metas-use-of-instagram-photos-raises-questions/), highlighting the tension between AI capability advancement and European regulatory frameworks.

**Decentralization vs. Monopolization of AI Development**: [Yann LeCun emphasized that distributed AI development could create millions of jobs, noting that Hugging Face has grown from hundreds of thousands to 15 million AI builders](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2074172096536555943), suggesting job creation rather than displacement if AI remains decentralized rather than monopolized by a few companies. This theme evolved across the week as [Andrew Ng and Yann LeCun advocated for protecting open-source AI development against potential government restrictions](https://x.com/AndrewYNg/status/2075271586400403567), framing permissionless innovation and open-source models as critical to maintaining competitive advantage and preventing power concentration.

**Geopolitical AI Governance and Competitive Dynamics**: [Alexander Stubb signalled that artificial intelligence is now a core topic in high-level diplomatic discussions, with EU and Chinese leadership explicitly addressing AI within frameworks of multilateral cooperation](https://x.com/alexstubb/status/2073845252922790118). The week revealed intensifying US-China-Europe competition: [China forced its biggest AI platforms to shut down humanlike chatbot personas](https://the-decoder.com/china-forces-its-biggest-ai-platforms-to-shut-down-humanlike-chatbot-personas/), [Chinese AI models gained ground with US companies due to cost advantages](https://the-decoder.com/chinese-ai-models-regularly-pass-30-percent-on-openrouter-as-cost-gap-widens/), and [Deepseek announced it was designing its own AI chip](https://the-decoder.com/deepseek-is-designing-its-own-ai-chip/), reflecting China's vertical integration strategy. Meanwhile, [China moved to restrict exports of its top AI models, potentially closing Europe's convenient shortcut of relying on cheap Chinese open-source models](https://the-decoder.com/china-eyes-export-curbs-on-its-top-ai-models-and-europe-is-caught-in-the-middle/). On the US side, [Sam Altman announced OpenAI's GPT-5.6 release with new products and cost improvements](https://x.com/sama/status/2075264378962907597), while [David Timis highlighted Elon Musk's acknowledgment of Anthropic's competitive leadership in frontier models](https://x.com/DavidTimis/status/2075367233786659015).

**AI Safety, Misuse, and Regulatory Gaps**: [AI Now Institute research revealed critical security vulnerabilities in Anthropic and OpenAI agents when used for defensive purposes](https://ainowinstitute.org/publications/double-agents), with [proof-of-concept exploits enabling remote code execution](https://ainowinstitute.org/publications/friendly-fire-exploit-brief). **AI Model Capabilities and Limitations**: Experts debated whether advanced AI systems imply omnipotence or have fundamental constraints. [Gary Marcus argued that "superintelligence does not logically entail omnipotence,"](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2076105975946232097) challenging inflated claims, while [Ramez Naam noted that "no matter how high quality your tokens are, they cannot turn lead into gold"](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2076106404125925565), emphasizing physical and logical limits. Conversely, [Sam Altman claimed that "AI has been net job-creating" contrary to earlier pessimistic predictions](https://x.com/sama/status/2076036901824532530), suggesting the economic integration of AI may differ from forecasts.

**European AI Research and Startup Ecosystem**: [Cees Snoek highlighted emerging AI research from ELLIS Amsterdam reaching international venues like ICML](https://x.com/cgmsnoek/status/2073794089191932230), demonstrating the Netherlands' continued contribution to cutting-edge AI development. ### Solvinity National Security Review Faces Judicial Scrutiny
The Dutch government's May decision to block Solvinity's acquisition by a US buyer on national security grounds is facing judicial scrutiny in Rotterdam's district court, with the company arguing that the state's review was procedurally deficient and incomplete. This case exemplifies an emerging tension in Dutch AI policy between protecting critical infrastructure and deterring foreign investment in the startup ecosystem, with the outcome likely setting precedent for how broadly "public interest" concerns are applied to technology acquisitions. **Tue 7 Jul** · [NOS Nieuws](https://nos.nl/l/2621945)
