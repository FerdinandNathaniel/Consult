The week revealed a fundamental tension in the global AI investment landscape: while US tech giants continue massive infrastructure spending ($200B+ combined CapEx), [expert scrutiny of AI company valuations and financial sustainability has intensified](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2115644636723327128), with [OpenAI raising only $11 billion of its $30 billion target](https://the-decoder.com/openai-raises-just-11-billion-of-its-30-billion-target-as-big-investors-lose-their-ai-appetite/) and [Anthropic's IPO filing revealing $800 billion in long-term compute commitments alongside mounting financial obligations](https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-opens-its-books-for-wall-street-showing-record-revenue-and-mounting-obligations/). ## What Were Followed Accounts Discussing This Week?

Expert discussions coalesced around four interconnected themes affecting the AI startup and investment ecosystem:

**AI Financial Sustainability and Valuation Risk** dominated discourse. [Gary Marcus and others highlighted signs of an AI investment bubble deflating](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2114517991568122077), with [debt markets tightening access to funding for AI companies lacking independent revenue streams](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2114517991568122077). [Meta's credit downgrade due to heavy capex and delayed AI monetization](https://x.com/Schuldensuehner/status/2116296678529523747) signaled broader financial stress, while [aggregate free cash flow for Microsoft, Amazon, Meta, and Google turned negative for the first time in decades](https://x.com/DavidTimis/status/2116801925043646826).

**Open-Source AI and European Capability Development** emerged as a counterweight to US dominance. [Thomas Wolf highlighted that open-weight models succeeded where OpenAI and Anthropic's systems failed in AI safety research](https://x.com/Thom_Wolf/status/2114840750936436837), while [Chinese open-weight models (Nanbeige) now compete with proprietary alternatives](https://x.com/Thom_Wolf/status/2114638463601484). **AI Infrastructure Economics and Memory Constraints** shifted focus from compute to supply-chain bottlenecks. [UBS analysis showed that meeting 2030 agentic AI buildout targets would require 282EB of DRAM—3.5x current industry estimates](https://x.com/DavidTimis/status/2116544080029049316), suggesting memory, not processing power, is the binding constraint. [US tech firms are increasingly raising capital through European debt markets](https://x.com/DavidTimis/status/2115199900929438072), creating competitive pressures for European startups accessing capital.

**AI Safety, Governance, and Regulatory Compliance** gained urgency. [Gary Marcus and others raised concerns about autonomous AI agents with internet access posing security threats](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2114318810568159577), while [Anthropic's EU AI Act compliance implementation revealed watermark loopholes on older Claude models](https://the-decoder.com/anthropics-watermark-rollout-leaves-a-loophole-on-two-older-claude-models/). [AI Now Institute roundtable discussions pushed the conversation toward corporate accountability and economic harms](https://ainowinstitute.org/news/what-should-the-rules-for-a-i-companies-actually-be-we-asked-the-experts), and [Google DeepMind's PlanAssist demonstrated practical AI deployment in UK public administration](https://x.com/demishassabis/status/2116651775017275581), signaling growing interest in AI's regulatory and administrative applications.
