# Weekly Round-Up — Week 38, 2026
*Based on 7 daily briefings · 14 September to 20 September 2026*

## Summary of the Week

European AI policy and startup investment dynamics shifted markedly this week as regulatory momentum collided with industry self-regulation skepticism and geopolitical competition intensified. [Von der Leyen signalled EU commitment to frontier AI governance](https://www.politico.eu/article/von-der-leyen-to-invite-ai-leaders-to-support-efforts-to-pace-the-frontier/?utm_source=RSS_Feed&utm_medium=RSS&utm_campaign=RSS_Syndication), inviting major AI labs to discuss "pacing" development, while simultaneously [the EU KIDS Act was adopted to restrict social media and AI systems' access to minors](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/eu-kids-act-restrict-social-media-platforms-access-children-eu), reflecting a regulatory tightening across digital services. However, [industry leaders including Nvidia's Jensen Huang and OpenAI executives publicly opposed binding AI regulation](https://www.politico.eu/article/jensen-huang-dont-regulate-ai-like-social-media/?utm_source=RSS_Feed&utm_medium=RSS&utm_campaign=RSS_Syndication), creating a widening gap between EU regulatory ambitions and US industry resistance. Most significantly for the Dutch ecosystem, [Dutch AI infrastructure startup Boxd secured $2 million in pre-seed funding from BlueYard Capital](https://tech.eu/2026/09/15/boxd-raises-2m-to-build-cloud-infrastructure-for-ai-coding-agents/) to develop cloud infrastructure for AI coding agents, demonstrating that international venture capital continues to flow toward specialized Dutch AI infrastructure plays despite regulatory uncertainty. Underlying these developments, [critical concerns emerged about AI safety governance credibility](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2100075459929346422), with experts questioning whether industry self-regulation and "independent" evaluators can credibly assess frontier AI risks, and [internal OpenAI and Microsoft documents revealed admissions that AI training on copyrighted material constitutes "the largest theft of labor in human history,"](https://the-decoder.com/ai-training-built-on-fair-use-looks-shaky-when-the-companies-own-people-call-it-astonishing-theft/) exposing significant legal and reputational vulnerabilities for major AI companies.

## What Were Followed Accounts Discussing This Week?

**AI Safety Governance and Regulatory Capture Concerns** dominated expert discourse throughout the week. [Gary Marcus and Yann LeCun raised concerns about conflicts of interest in AI safety evaluation](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2099656197901353016), particularly around Anthropic's partnerships with third-party evaluators like METR and Accenture, characterizing arrangements as lacking genuine independence and representing a "regulatory capture machine." [Marcus advocated for FDA-style AI regulation and independent external auditors](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2100368877956010028) rather than industry self-regulation, while simultaneously [highlighting concrete cybersecurity failures at major AI companies](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2100794507138871801), including OpenAI's repeated security breaches where hackers gained access to employee accounts and internal codebases within 72 hours. This reflected a broader skepticism about whether AI companies' public safety commitments lack credibility and whether policy decisions are being driven by narrative rather than technical facts.

**European AI Strategy and Fragmentation** emerged as a secondary theme. [Arthur Mensch critiqued Europe's proliferation of overlapping AI initiatives](https://x.com/arthurmensch/status/2099579960155390063) (transformative-ai.eu, accelerate-europe.org, aichampions.eu), suggesting strategic incoherence, while [Max Welling and collaborators published a "Transformative AI Strategy for Europe"](https://x.com/wellingmax/status/2099452272337940766) addressing prosperity, sovereignty, and security through supply chain coordination. [David Timis argued that policymakers must treat AI's economic effects and power redistribution seriously](https://x.com/DavidTimis/status/2099887596126048597), noting that AI benefits depend on countries' position in the supply chain and digital readiness, and that [sovereign AI strategy requires managing dependencies strategically rather than eliminating them](https://x.com/DavidTimis/status/2099836320415674799).

**Geopolitical AI Competition and Deal-Making** intensified as a recurring theme. [Gary Marcus proposed an unconventional US-China AI cooperation framework as an alternative to an uncontrolled race](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2099533789726921190), while [Ryan Hass noted China's concerns that AI could undermine CCP political control](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2099600517057656463), indicating Beijing views AI governance through a security and sovereignty lens. [China flatly rejected US AI safety warnings as "fearmongering" designed to lock in American advantage](https://the-decoder.com/china-fires-back-at-u-s-ai-safety-warnings-calling-them-fearmongering-to-lock-in-american-advantage/), while [China's security minister called for faster AI infrastructure buildout rather than slowdowns](https://the-decoder.com/china-fires-back-at-u-s-ai-safety-warnings-calling-them-fearmongering-to-lock-in-american-advantage/). [Holger Zschaepitz reported that Goldman Sachs' AI pair trade suffered its worst day since January 2025](https://x.com/Schuldensuehner/status/2099802854315745756), triggered by debate over slowing AI development, signaling how crowded and fragile the AI investment consensus has become.

**AI Development Pace and Competitive Pressure** revealed contradictions between stated safety commitments and actual behavior. [Gary Marcus argued that despite calls for AI slowdowns, competitive and economic pressures will prevent meaningful deceleration](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2101128959026954676), noting Anthropic's reported plans to release new models despite founder Dario Amodei's public calls for slowdown. [Yann LeCun countered with empirical harm data, noting only 362 tracked LLM incidents among 1.2 billion users (0.000030% rate)](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2101485259791405197), while [Gary Marcus highlighted that major AI models provide wrong answers on financial queries 57% of the time on average](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2101467536880881180), raising questions about deployment readiness. [David Timis reported that hyperscalers have locked in $2.7 trillion in future compute commitments](https://x.com/DavidTimis/status/2101432229402657066), with Google alone committing $811 billion, suggesting the AI capex boom is structurally entrenched.

**Open-Source AI Safety and Transparency** emerged as a counterpoint to concentrated risk narratives. [Thomas Wolf collaborated with Base Labs and Goodfire AI to establish safety and interpretability standards for open-source models](https://x.com/Thom_Wolf/status/2100327168421277779), emphasizing that openness enables greater safety visibility and research tooling compared to closed systems. [Yann LeCun argued that AI risk concentration among powerful labs makes open-source AI development a mitigation strategy rather than a threat](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2100984195921174646), contrasting with narratives that treat all AI proliferation as equally risky.
