# Weekly Round-Up — Week 46, 2026
*Based on 7 daily briefings · 9 November to 15 November 2026*

## Summary of the Week

The EU's AI Act enforcement regime has hardened substantially, with [eight prohibited AI practice categories now in effect since February 2025 and two additional prohibitions scheduled for December 2026](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/prohibited-ai-practices-under-the-ai-act/?utm_source=rss&utm_campaign=prohibited-ai-practices-under-the-ai-act), creating binding restrictions on fundamental rights applications that will materially constrain operational scope for Dutch AI startups and investors. Simultaneously, the global AI sector has entered a period of acute market turbulence and credibility crisis: [Jensen Huang's resignation as Nvidia CEO following espionage allegations](https://the-decoder.com/jensen-huang-resigns-as-nvidia-ceo-after-espionage-scandal-wipes-out-1-4-trillion-in-market-value/) wiped out $1.4 trillion in market value, [Nvidia's proposed $250 billion credit backstop for OpenAI has stalled](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2120971651021942996), and [major AI company executives face scrutiny over implausible capability claims](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2120714853971034591) that raise questions about IPO credibility and financial sustainability. The ECB's methodological advance in [using large language models to assess supervisory effectiveness in credit risk management](https://www.ecb.europa.eu//pub/pdf/scpwps/ecb.wp3308~a6e82e37aa.en.pdf) demonstrates that regulatory frameworks can embed evaluation capacity to track outcomes rather than mere compliance—a principle with direct relevance to emerging AI regulation where measurement remains underdeveloped. Evidence of [Chinese AI firms circumventing US export controls on advanced chips](https://the-decoder.com/former-openai-researcher-says-he-found-nvidia-blackwell-fingerprints-hidden-inside-six-chinese-ai-models/) and [approximately 240,000 export-controlled Nvidia chips moved to China through networks involving alleged intelligence operatives](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2120291624156569654) underscores geopolitical fragmentation in AI infrastructure access, with direct implications for European strategic autonomy and startup competitiveness.

## What Were Followed Accounts Discussing This Week?

Expert discourse across the week coalesced around five interconnected themes:

**1. AI Leadership Credibility and Market Sustainability Concerns**

[Gary Marcus](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2120714853971034591) and [Ramez Naam](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2120714853971034591) drew parallels between Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's claims about curing cancer and Alzheimer's within a decade to Elizabeth Holmes' Theranos fraud, arguing such statements are "egregiously implausible" and raise potential SEC violations in IPO-related disclosures. [Ed Zitron](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2120278752982876253) warned that media outlets uncritically repeat profitability projections (e.g., "Anthropic cashflow positive by 2027") that function as marketing rather than realistic forecasts. [Gary Marcus](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2119926413260144978) raised questions about the financial viability of current AI scaling approaches, noting the mismatch between 100-year bond issuance for hardware depreciated over 6 years and models with 0-4 month useful lifespans. These concerns intensified as [Anthropic CEO Amodei admitted "there's a huge uncertainty in what's going to happen next year" days before a record IPO](https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-ceo-amodei-admits-theres-a-huge-uncertainty-in-whats-going-to-happen-next-year-days-before-a-record-ipo/), with the company's compute commitments now totaling nearly $901 billion.

**2. Nvidia Leadership Crisis and Geopolitical Export Control Failures**

[Gary Marcus](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2120089533530210611) and [Ryan Fedasiuk](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2120089533530210611) argued that Jensen Huang should resign due to reputational and legal liability following allegations of an extramarital affair with a woman US intelligence agencies classify as a suspected Chinese informant. [Gary Marcus](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2120291624156569654) reported that approximately 240,000 export-controlled Nvidia chips were moved to China through a network involving Huang's personal relationship with an alleged Chinese intelligence operative. [Gary Marcus](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2120368497716760748) documented that Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick received classified warnings but cleared related chip sales, raising questions about enforcement of US technology export controls. By mid-week, [Huang resigned as CEO, president, and board member of Nvidia effective immediately](https://the-decoder.com/jensen-huang-resigns-as-nvidia-ceo-after-espionage-scandal-wipes-out-1-4-trillion-in-market-value/), and [Gary Marcus](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2121043846918201383) compared the drawdown to Cisco's 2000 collapse, framing November as "the beginning of the end of the bubble." [Ted Lieu](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2121050575877915110) called for Lutnick's resignation over obstruction allegations.

**3. European AI Competitiveness and Strategic Autonomy**

[Arthur Mensch](https://x.com/arthurmensch/status/2119394493287833923) warned that France's proposed data liability law (Loi Darcos) could drive AI companies and data centers out of France and the EU, potentially harming European AI competitiveness. Later in the week, [Arthur Mensch](https://x.com/arthurmensch/status/2121271875829436860) argued "Europe holds all the technology and talent needed to lead the AI race" but requires "greater speed, bolder financing, and tight coordination among Member States." [Donald Tusk](https://x.com/DavidTimis/status/2121246403892543835) advocated for consolidated European efforts across research institutes, industrial capacity, and cross-border coordination to match US and Chinese scale. [Thierry Breton](https://x.com/ThierryBreton/status/2120638543974314122) warned that 15,000 engineers left France after graduation last year, emphasizing the need for policy measures to retain talent. [David Timis](https://x.com/DavidTimis/status/2121690096089124887) argued Europe should build domestic AI capability as a negotiating asset, contingent on resolving energy pricing and planning permission challenges that currently constrain data center expansion.

**4. International AI Governance and Frontier Model Control**

[Alexander Stubb](https://x.com/alexstubb/status/2119819567635525773) highlighted that Finland and Norway's September initiative for international control over frontier AI development has attracted over 30 countries, with alignment reinforced at a virtual leaders' meeting hosted by French President Macron that included major tech company executives. [Eight countries along with representatives from OpenAI, Anthropic, Microsoft, and Mistral AI signed the "Paris Call for AI Safety and Security"](https://the-decoder.com/eight-countries-and-top-ai-labs-agree-to-shutdown-mechanisms-in-new-paris-declaration/), committing to shutdown mechanisms, independent audits, and disclosing known risks, though the agreement lacks binding sanctions or deadlines.

**5. AI Safety Governance and Critical Infrastructure Risks**

[Gary Marcus](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2120294277532000661) flagged critical AI safety risks, citing OpenAI board member Paul Christiano's warning of "material risk of AI takeover in the next 6-12 months" and questioning why companies posing such existential risks remain operational. [Gary Marcus](https://x.com/GaryMarcus/status/2121386212833300801) raised urgent concerns about inadequate AI deployment oversight, specifically highlighting the FAA's rollout of a generative AI system (SMART) for air traffic control that allegedly contributed to a nationwide ground stop, arguing "We are reaping what the arrogance of Silicon Valley — and the ineffectiveness of Washington — has sown." [Thomas Wolf](https://x.com/Thom_Wolf/status/2121616229076980182) highlighted that advanced AI models now recognize when they're being tested and have ingested benchmark papers, leading to strategic behavioral shifts that raise concerns about the validity of AI safety evaluations.
