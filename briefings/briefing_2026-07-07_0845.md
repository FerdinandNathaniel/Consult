# Executive Summary

The Dutch government's May decision to block Solvinity's acquisition by a US buyer on national security grounds is facing judicial scrutiny, with the company arguing before Rotterdam's district court that the state's review was procedurally deficient and incomplete. This case exemplifies an emerging tension in Dutch AI policy: the tension between protecting critical infrastructure and national security interests against the risk of deterring foreign investment in the startup ecosystem. The outcome will likely set precedent for how broadly "public interest" concerns are applied to technology acquisitions, particularly relevant as the Netherlands positions itself as a competitive hub for AI investment while managing legitimate security vulnerabilities in sensitive sectors.
