# Executive Summary

Investor behaviour is shifting toward treating trustworthy AI as a material business factor rather than a peripheral governance concern, according to the OECD AI Policy Observatory. This reorientation carries direct implications for the Netherlands' startup investment climate, as it signals that capital allocation decisions will increasingly hinge on AI governance frameworks, risk management practices, and compliance infrastructure. For Dutch startups and policymakers, this development underscores the competitive advantage of embedding trustworthiness standards early in product development and the corresponding risk of capital flight to jurisdictions with clearer AI governance signals. The shift also suggests that investor due diligence on AI systems will become a standard gate in funding rounds, potentially reshaping which Dutch ventures attract institutional backing.
