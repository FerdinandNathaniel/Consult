## Summary of the Week

The European Commission's publication of [EN 18286:2026 in the Official Journal](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/first-harmonised-standard-supporting-ai-act-published-official-journal) establishes the first harmonised standard for quality management systems under the AI Act, removing a critical implementation bottleneck for high-risk AI system providers across the EU and directly lowering regulatory uncertainty for Dutch and European AI startups. Simultaneously, [ECB research reveals that banks across the euro area are systematically underestimating default risk for AI-exposed borrowers](https://www.ecb.europa.eu//pub/pdf/scpwps/ecb.wp3302~7ff8a286bc.en.pdf), with probability-of-default estimates declining by approximately 5 percent following the ChatGPT inflection point—a credit market mispricing that creates a near-term financing window for AI ventures but coincides with tightening compliance requirements that will differentiate viable from unsustainable business models. Dutch photonics startup [Photon Bridge secured €10 million to address a critical AI infrastructure bottleneck in optical connectivity](https://tech.eu/2026/10/21/dutch-photonics-startup-photon-bridge-raises-eur10m-to-tackle-ais-looming-laser-bottleneck/), signalling investor recognition of the Netherlands' potential as a hub for solving hardware-layer constraints in AI infrastructure. However, the week also surfaced mounting concerns about AI investment sustainability: corporate token spending growth collapsed from 42 percent to 0.2 percent, return on invested capital at hyperscalers fell from 38 to 20 percent, and only 13 percent of companies report real business value from AI investments despite 75 percent of CEOs planning to raise technology budgets. European fiscal fragility—particularly France's 10-year yield spread over Germany widening to 175 basis points and Germany's composite PMI falling to 52.8—creates macroeconomic headwinds that could constrain venture funding availability across the continent.

### EN 18286:2026 Harmonised Standard for AI Act Compliance
The European Commission published [EN 18286:2026 - Artificial intelligence - Quality management system for EU AI Act regulatory purposes](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/first-harmonised-standard-supporting-ai-act-published-official-journal) in the Official Journal, developed by CEN and CENELEC. This first harmonised standard provides a clear legal pathway for providers of high-risk AI systems to comply with quality management system requirements, removing a critical implementation bottleneck and lowering regulatory uncertainty for Dutch and European AI startups navigating market entry. The standard signals the EU's transition from legislative framework to enforcement phase, making compliance costs and timelines more calculable for AI firms.

### ECB Research: Banks Systematically Underestimate Default Risk for AI-Exposed Borrowers
The [ECB Working Paper "Headwind or tailwind? How banks interpret borrowers' AI exposure"](https://www.ecb.europa.eu//pub/pdf/scpwps/ecb.wp3302~7ff8a286bc.en.pdf) reveals that banks across the euro area lower assessed default risk for borrowers in highly AI-exposed industries by approximately 5 percent following the ChatGPT inflection point. The reassessment is weaker for labor-intensive firms and stronger among lenders with pre-existing industry expertise, reflecting interpretive uncertainty around a general-purpose technology whose firm-level distribution remains difficult to assess in real time. This credit market mispricing creates a near-term financing window for AI ventures but coincides with tightening compliance requirements that will differentiate viable from unsustainable business models.

*Tue 20 Oct · [ECB Working Papers](https://www.ecb.europa.eu//pub/pdf/scpwps/ecb.wp3302~7ff8a286bc.en.pdf)*
