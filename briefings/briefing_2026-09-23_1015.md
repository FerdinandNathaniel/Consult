# Executive Summary

The Netherlands is positioning itself as a critical node in European AI infrastructure development through coordinated public and private sector action targeting the sector's most acute technical bottleneck: energy efficiency. The National Agency for Disruptive Innovation (NADI) and Germany's SPRIND have jointly committed €40 million to the AI-Native Chip Design Challenge, selecting seven teams to develop energy-efficient AI chips—a direct response to the escalating power consumption crisis that threatens both the economic viability and climate sustainability of AI deployment. This public investment is complemented by significant private capital flowing into adjacent hardware layers: Morphotonics, a Dutch deeptech firm, has closed a €40 million Series B to scale manufacturing technology for AI-integrated optical devices, indicating investor confidence in Dutch capabilities across the hardware stack. The convergence of NADI's launch with these funding announcements suggests the Dutch government is deliberately leveraging its emerging innovation infrastructure to capture value in foundational AI hardware rather than competing in software or applications alone.
